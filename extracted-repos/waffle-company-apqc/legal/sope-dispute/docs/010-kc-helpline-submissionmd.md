# KCHelpLine Form Submission

## Supplier Code of Conduct Concern - Non-Payment to Subcontractor

**Supplier:** SOPE Web Technologies Pty Ltd (ABN 34 151 358 511), Melbourne, Australia

**My Role:** Subcontractor providing SharePoint/Microsoft 365 consulting on Kimberly-Clark Australia projects

---

## Summary

I am reporting a potential breach of Kimberly-Clark's Supplier Code of Conduct requirement that suppliers "extend principles of fair and honest dealing to all others with whom they do business, including employees, subcontractors."

---

## Project History

From late 2024, I worked on the Kimberly-Clark Australia SharePoint project as SOPE's assigned consultant. I had direct working relationships with K-C staff including Hourie Demirjian and Lauren Crawley, participating in requirements meetings, developing technical solutions, and delivering a UAT version of the system.

On June 10, 2025, SOPE's Professional Services Manager Ashley Kenny wrote: "Our recommended path is to approve the 12-day proposal provided so we can schedule Cameron to the engagement."

---

## Pattern of Unapproved Work

Throughout 2025, K-C staff repeatedly requested my involvement in meetings, requirements discussions, and technical consultations. I repeatedly cautioned that time needed to be approved before requesting my involvement.

On June 18, 2025, I documented this pattern in an email to Lauren Crawley:

> "Time spent beyond the approved quota were not initiated by me - yet that time has been invested. The lessons from February, when efforts to 'push for the outcome' exceeded agreed time allocations, remain very clear to me."

> "Since UAT, I honestly can't count how many times I've raised this, including: before meetings were requested, before meetings were scheduled, during meetings, as the top action item arising from those meetings..."

> "I understand that my repeated insistence on this point - or my reluctance to continue investing time under the current, unapproved conditions - may have been perceived as disengagement."

K-C also repeatedly requested requotes, revised estimates, and impact analysis - inadvertently adding to the problem. K-C was entitled to make these requests, but there was a gap in the approval process. The understanding was that this consultation investment would be recouped during the delivery phase.

On June 26, 2025, Lauren Crawley acknowledged: "I think in hindsight the project wasn't approached the right way or with the right engagement setup (time & materials)."

---

## SOPE Discouraged Transparency

After my June 18 email to Lauren, I was rebuked by Ashley Kenny: "we do not speak to clients like that."

SOPE preferred K-C not know the true situation regarding unapproved work.

---

## Work Approved While Account in Bad Standing

On October 23, 2025, Ashley Kenny confirmed: "KCC - The works are approved (not sure if you saw my email about it last week), so we need to get availability for a workshop please?"

At this time, SOPE's account was already in bad standing due to unpaid invoices on other projects. I had communicated to SOPE that they needed to get their accounts in order. Despite this, SOPE approved new K-C work and requested my availability.

When I provided availability, I declined other client opportunities to remain available for K-C.

---

## Reassignment and "Unavailability" Claim

On November 14, 2025 - just three weeks after confirming "works are approved" - SOPE CEO Trent Allday emailed:

> "Regarding the Kimberly-Clark project, this one been reassigned to another team member due to the client's time constraints and the urgency to move forward."

This occurred without prior discussion, despite my institutional knowledge from months of involvement, and despite my having provided availability for the approved work.

SOPE later characterised my conduct as "unavailability." This is misleading. I was available - I had provided availability when work was approved. What I was unwilling to do was continue providing services on credit to a client in arrears. This is corporate diligence, not unavailability.

By reassigning delivery, SOPE broke the mechanism that would have compensated the unapproved consultation work. The approval gap was never closed.

---

## Why This Is More Than a Contract Dispute

This is not simply a payment dispute between supplier and subcontractor. This concerns K-C's supply chain integrity.

**K-C unknowingly received work from an unpaid subcontractor.** K-C's project benefited from months of my consultation and expertise - labor that SOPE now refuses to pay for.

**SOPE kept K-C in the dark.** SOPE chose to ignore the payment problems under the pretense of "we cannot keep clients waiting." When I attempted transparency, SOPE rebuked me. K-C was not informed of the true situation.

**K-C's project continuity was disrupted.** The consultant K-C worked with for months - who had institutional knowledge and delivered the UAT system - was suddenly replaced.

**Pattern risk.** If SOPE operates this way systematically, K-C's other engagements may be affected by quality issues or subcontractor turnover.

**Reputational risk.** K-C's supply chain includes a company that does not pay its workers.

---

## The Pattern

1. SOPE allows invoices to fall into arrears
2. Subcontractor communicates: get your accounts in order
3. Despite bad standing, SOPE approves new work (October 23: "works are approved")
4. Subcontractor provides availability, declining other opportunities
5. Subcontractor performs consultation work, expecting to recoup during delivery
6. When subcontractor exercises caution, SOPE calls this "unavailability"
7. SOPE reassigns delivery (November 14), breaking the compensation mechanism
8. SOPE refuses payment, claiming no agreement existed

---

## Contract Misuse

SOPE cites a **Location** clause: "Work will be performed at your residential address, and at the offices of the Company and its Customers', as agreed between yourself, the Company and customers."

This specifies **where** work is performed. SOPE misinterprets it to mean they can withdraw assignments with no payment obligation.

The same contract states: "Payment will be made by the Company within 30 days... for work undertaken."

Work was undertaken. K-C requested it. SOPE benefited from it. The contract entitles me to payment.
