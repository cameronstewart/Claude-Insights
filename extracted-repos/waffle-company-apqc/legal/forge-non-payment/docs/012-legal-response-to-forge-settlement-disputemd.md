## Formal Response to Settlement Proposal

Dear Counsel,

We acknowledge receipt of your client's correspondence dated 15 August 2025 regarding the disputed invoice B-412824-7 for the amount of $10,877.63 (inclusive of GST).

Despite our legitimate attempts to resolve these issues professionally, we have instead received another series of threats, seemingly in keeping with the overall culture of Forge.

This response addresses the substantive legal and contractual issues raised, supported by comprehensive project documentation and recorded client communications.

## 1. Contractual Framework - Time & Materials Basis

### Explicit Agreement Documentation
Your client's assertion that this was a fixed-price contract is factually incorrect and contradicted by executed documentation.

**Quote QU-0360 dated 24 January 2025** explicitly states: **"ESTIMATED ON A TIME AND MATERIALS BASIS"**. This was expressly discussed at the time as a historical quote (more than 6 months old) and that The Waffle Company would do its best to honor it, but likely that due to additional requirements and complexity, this would be a starting point only to commence the project. This was proceeded with and acknowledged by Roger Joyce, who approved in response within 20 June.

This contractual foundation was:
- Clearly documented in the signed agreement
- Expressly acknowledged by your client throughout the engagement
- Confirmed by Adrian Trimmer on 24 July 2025: *"I am paying you by the hour, you work when I want, do what I want and in the way I want. To work in any other way would be wasting time"*

## 2. Client Acknowledgement of Cost Overrun

Contrary to your assertions, your client was fully aware of and accepted the cost overrun through documented correspondence:

**Email dated 7 July 2025:**
- **Cameron Stewart (The Waffle Co)**: *"I have just checked in the log of hours, we are sitting @ 42.5 so far"*
- **Your Client's Response**: *"Feel free to call me this afternoon to talk hours/$"*

This exchange demonstrates clear awareness and acceptance of hours beyond the initial estimate. Despite agreeing to discuss the cost implications by phone, when the time came to be able to provide more accurate estimates, calls and messages were left, but not returned, nor provided any explanation as to why - clear indication that additional effort known.

Further meetings, as requested by Forge, often at short notice by Nina Wilson, which members of The Waffle Company attended, with the exception of sending note taking delegate for meetings required by Forge outside of standard business hours, eg (5am meeting).

## 3. Quality of Work - Explicit Client Confirmation

Your allegation regarding work quality is contradicted by your client's own statements during the 24 July 2025 meeting.

When expressly questioned about quality concerns, **Adrian Trimmer explicitly stated**:
*"No one's saying that it was not built to the specifications."*

This explicit confirmation establishes that the deliverables met the agreed specifications.

## 4. Data Responsibility - Client Acknowledgement

Your client expressly accepted responsibility for data accuracy issues through multiple documented instances:

### FLS Column Implementation Warning
The Waffle Company explicitly warned about reliability issues:
*"I asked about the FLS field before - there are only 49 Opps with data in that field, so I would expect that reporting is wildly inaccurate if we base it of that."*

This was based on lessons learnt during previous projects with Forge. Despite this professional advice about potential data accuracy issues, your client chose to proceed with this methodology.

### Explicit Responsibility Acceptance
**Email dated 21 July 2025** - Adrian Trimmer (Forge): *"the correctness of the data is on us."*

**Meeting dated 24 July 2025**: *"Adrian Trimmer has prescribed and he has specified that if the data is not right, that is on the onus on you to fix that"* - outcome of the meeting minuted.

These statements were made without contradiction, establishing your client's explicit acceptance of responsibility for data accuracy.

### Timeliness and Access Methods
Against The Waffle Company's recommendation and industry best practice, Forge insisted on data access methods as specifically instructed by them, despite our concerns about timeliness and efficiency. At key moments this resulted in delays.

Notably, this included Forge's insistence, through Senior Manager Adrian Trimmer and Project Manager Nina Wilson, to contact staff member Madeline Joyce whilst on medical leave, encouraging breach of Right to Disconnect laws to obtain necessary data access. When refused, this was promoted as an example of unreliability - despite the timeliness issues being based on decisions Forge made, with risks known and acknowledged by Adrian Trimmer.

## 5. Fitness for Purpose - Premature Termination

Your assertion regarding work standards must be considered in the context of your client's unilateral termination of the engagement on 28 July 2025:

*"We have made a decision to stop working together, we don't feel it is working and getting the result that we need."*

Claims regarding fitness for purpose cannot be sustained when the client terminates the engagement before project completion. The deliverables at termination represented work-in-progress, not final deliverables.

Your client's own acknowledgement confirms this: *"send through any remaining invoices and we can review against the agreements we had."*
