Introduction

This report provides an overview of the Corporate Information Services
sector and examines the role of the Service Line Owner within this
domain. Corporate Information Services represents a specialised area
within the broader information profession that focuses on managing
organisational knowledge assets, digital content, and information
systems within business, government, and not-for-profit settings. As
organisations increasingly rely on digital platforms and face growing
regulatory requirements around information governance, this sector has
become essential to operational effectiveness and strategic
decision-making.

The report is structured to first examine the Corporate Information
Services sector, including its historical development, purpose,
stakeholders, and the types of information and formats typically
managed. The second section provides a detailed analysis of the Service
Line Owner role, including responsibilities, required competencies, and
the skills necessary for success in this position.

The Corporate Information Services Sector

Historical Development and Context

Corporate Information Services has evolved significantly from its
origins in traditional records management and corporate libraries. The
sector emerged in the mid-twentieth century as organisations recognised
the need for systematic approaches to managing business records and
providing research services to support decision-making (Lee et al.,
2020). Early corporate information centres functioned primarily as
specialised libraries, maintaining collections of industry publications,
technical reports, and regulatory documents.

The digital revolution of the 1990s and 2000s fundamentally transformed
this sector. The introduction of Enterprise Content Management (ECM)
systems, document management platforms, and later cloud-based solutions
such as Microsoft SharePoint created new demands for information
professionals who could bridge technical systems and business
requirements (Svärd, 2017). This technological shift repositioned
information management from a support function to a strategic capability
essential for organisational competitiveness.

More recently, the sector has been shaped by regulatory developments
including privacy legislation, freedom of information requirements, and
industry-specific compliance frameworks. Organisations in sectors such
as energy, finance, and healthcare face particularly stringent
requirements around information retention, disposal, and security (Khan
et al., 2023). The emergence of artificial intelligence and machine
learning technologies is now driving further evolution, as organisations
seek to leverage their information assets for automated decision-making
while maintaining appropriate governance controls (Luna-Reyes &
Harrison, 2023).

Purpose and Activities

The fundamental purpose of Corporate Information Services is to ensure
that organisational information is captured, organised, preserved, and
made accessible to support business operations, regulatory compliance,
and strategic decision-making (Momoti, 2023). This encompasses several
interconnected activities that together form a comprehensive information
management capability.

Information governance represents a core activity, establishing
policies, standards, and procedures for how information is created,
classified, retained, and disposed of throughout its lifecycle. This
includes developing classification schemes, retention schedules, and
security frameworks aligned with regulatory requirements and business
needs (Olaniyi et al., 2023). Governance activities also extend to data
quality management, ensuring that organisational information remains
accurate, complete, and fit for purpose.

Content and document management activities focus on the practical
implementation of governance frameworks through technology platforms.
This includes configuring and maintaining ECM systems, developing
information architectures that support findability and usability, and
managing migrations as organisations transition between platforms
(Yerneni & Yerneni Khaga, 2025). Service delivery activities encompass
user support, training, and consultation to help business units
effectively manage their information within established frameworks.

Stakeholders and Clients

Corporate Information Services operates at the intersection of multiple
stakeholder groups with varying needs and expectations. Executive
leadership requires assurance that information risks are managed
appropriately and that information assets support strategic objectives.
Legal and compliance teams depend on robust retention and disposal
practices to meet regulatory obligations and support litigation
readiness.

Operational business units represent the primary client base, requiring
functional systems that enable efficient document creation,
collaboration, and retrieval without imposing excessive administrative
burden. Information technology teams are key partners, providing the
technical infrastructure and development capabilities that underpin
information management platforms. External stakeholders including
regulators, auditors, and business partners also influence requirements
through compliance expectations and collaborative information sharing
needs.

Types of Information Managed

Corporate Information Services manages diverse information types
reflecting the breadth of organisational activities. Operational records
including contracts, correspondence, project documentation, and
financial records form the foundation of most corporate collections.
Technical and engineering documentation is particularly significant in
industries such as energy, manufacturing, and construction, where
accurate technical records support safety, maintenance, and regulatory
compliance.

Governance and compliance documentation including policies, procedures,
audit reports, and regulatory submissions represents another significant
category. Human resources information, including personnel files,
training records, and workplace safety documentation, carries specific
retention and privacy requirements. Many organisations also maintain
research and market intelligence collections to support strategic
planning and competitive positioning.

Information Formats

Contemporary Corporate Information Services manages information across
an expanding range of formats. Digital documents including word
processing files, spreadsheets, presentations, and PDF documents remain
predominant, typically managed through platforms such as Microsoft
SharePoint Online and enterprise document management systems (Ahmad et
al., 2023). Email and collaborative content from platforms such as
Microsoft Teams increasingly represents a significant and challenging
format category.

Structured data within databases and business systems requires
coordination with data governance frameworks and integration with
unstructured content management. Physical records including paper files,
maps, drawings, and legacy media formats persist in many organisations,
requiring hybrid management approaches. Audio-visual content including
training materials, recorded meetings, and multimedia communications
represents a growing format category with specific storage and access
requirements.

The Service Line Owner Role

Role Description

The Service Line Owner represents a senior information management
position responsible for the strategic direction, governance, and
operational delivery of information management services within an
organisation. This role typically sits within a digital, technology, or
corporate services division and provides centralised ownership of
information management capabilities, platforms, and policies (Ilori et
al., 2024).

Unlike traditional records manager or librarian roles that may focus on
specific collections or processes, the Service Line Owner takes an
enterprise perspective, coordinating across multiple platforms, business
units, and stakeholder groups. The role bridges strategic governance
responsibilities with operational service delivery, ensuring that
information management capabilities align with organisational objectives
while meeting the practical needs of business users.

Key Responsibilities and Tasks

Service Line Owners are responsible for developing and maintaining
information governance frameworks including policies, standards, and
procedures that guide how information is managed across the
organisation. This involves working with legal, compliance, and business
stakeholders to translate regulatory requirements and business needs
into practical governance instruments (Yebenes Serrano & Zorrilla,
2021).

Platform ownership represents a significant responsibility, encompassing
the strategic direction and functional configuration of information
management systems such as SharePoint, enterprise content management
platforms, and records management applications. The Service Line Owner
evaluates new technologies and features, determining their applicability
to organisational needs and coordinating implementation with technical
teams (Suleman et al., 2023).

Service delivery oversight involves managing the provision of
information management services to business units, typically through a
combination of direct support, self-service capabilities, and managed
service arrangements. This includes managing service requests,
consulting on information architecture and site design, and coordinating
migration and remediation projects. The role also encompasses
stakeholder engagement, building relationships with business leaders to
understand emerging requirements and communicate the value of
information management capabilities.

Required Skills

Effective Service Line Owners require a distinctive combination of
technical, analytical, and interpersonal skills. Technical skills
include proficiency with enterprise content management platforms,
understanding of information architecture principles, and familiarity
with governance and compliance tools such as Microsoft Purview (Ahmad et
al., 2023). While deep coding expertise is not typically required, the
ability to understand technical capabilities and constraints is
essential for effective platform decisions.

Analytical skills support the translation of business requirements into
information management solutions. This includes the ability to assess
current state environments, identify improvement opportunities, and
design solutions that balance user needs with governance requirements.
Communication and stakeholder management skills are critical, as the
role requires influencing without direct authority across diverse
stakeholder groups from executive leadership to operational staff
(Special Libraries Association, 2016).

Required Knowledge

Service Line Owners require knowledge across several domains.
Information management theory and practice provides the conceptual
foundation, including understanding of records management principles,
information lifecycle management, and metadata standards. Knowledge of
IT service management frameworks such as ITIL supports effective service
delivery and integration with broader technology operations (Ilori et
al., 2024).

Regulatory and compliance knowledge relevant to the organisation\'s
industry and jurisdiction is essential, particularly regarding records
retention, privacy, and information security requirements. Understanding
of change management principles supports the adoption aspects of
information management initiatives, recognising that technology
implementation represents only a portion of successful outcomes
(Karhapää et al., 2024).

Capabilities and Attributes

Beyond specific skills and knowledge, successful Service Line Owners
demonstrate particular capabilities and personal attributes. Strategic
thinking enables the role holder to position information management
initiatives within broader organisational objectives and anticipate
future requirements, including emerging technologies such as artificial
intelligence (Hanson et al., 2023). A service orientation ensures focus
on enabling business outcomes rather than imposing compliance for its
own sake.

Adaptability is essential given the rapid evolution of technology
platforms and regulatory requirements. The ability to balance governance
rigour with practical flexibility allows effective navigation of
competing stakeholder demands. Leadership capability, even without
formal authority, enables the Service Line Owner to drive change and
build coalitions of support for information management initiatives
across organisational boundaries.

Conclusion

Corporate Information Services represents a dynamic and strategically
important sector within the information profession. Evolving from
traditional records management and corporate library functions, the
sector now encompasses sophisticated digital content management,
governance frameworks, and emerging artificial intelligence
capabilities. The sector serves diverse stakeholders and manages
information across an expanding range of formats and platforms.

The Service Line Owner role exemplifies the contemporary information
professional working within this sector. Combining strategic governance
responsibilities with operational service delivery, the role requires a
distinctive blend of technical knowledge, analytical capability, and
interpersonal skills. As organisations continue to recognise information
as a strategic asset and face increasing regulatory scrutiny, the
importance of both the Corporate Information Services sector and roles
such as the Service Line Owner will continue to grow.
